Cleaning frequency should match how your office is actually used. Under-cleaning creates hygiene and presentation issues; over-cleaning wastes budget.

## Start with occupancy profile

Review:

- Daily headcount
- Client/visitor traffic
- Shared kitchen and amenities load
- Meeting room turnover

Higher usage means higher frequency in key zones.

## Baseline frequency model

- **Daily:** Amenities, kitchens, touch points, waste
- **2-3x weekly:** Workstations, floors, common zones
- **Weekly/periodic:** Internal glass, detail work, deep sanitation

Adjust by seasonality and peak periods.

## Common scheduling mistakes

1. One frequency for all zones
2. No distinction between peak and off-peak days
3. No review loop after occupancy changes

A written scope with zone-level frequency fixes these quickly.

## Newcastle office examples

A 12-person professional office with low visitor traffic may run **three-times-weekly** general cleaning with **daily** kitchen and amenities touch points. A 40-person customer-facing office in Newcastle CBD often needs **daily** presentation cleaning in reception and meeting rooms, with workstations on a **2–3x weekly** cycle.

Mixed tenancy buildings add complexity: base building standards may differ from suite-level expectations. Document who cleans what — carpets in common corridors versus inside the tenancy line — before you sign.

## How to review frequency quarterly

Occupancy changes faster than contracts. Schedule a quarterly review against:

- Sick-leave patterns and hygiene complaints
- Kitchen and amenities condition at end of day
- Client comments on presentation
- New hires or desk reconfiguration

If two or more indicators slip, adjust frequencies in the written scope rather than adding informal “extra cleans” that are hard to audit or cost.

## Building a scope your team can audit

Your scope should list each zone, task, product class (where relevant) and frequency in plain English. Photos or checklists for monthly reporting make it obvious what “done” looks like. That is especially useful when multiple managers or a committee oversees the contract.
